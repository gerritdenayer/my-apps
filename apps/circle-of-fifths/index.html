How the circle works (1 minute read)
  • Clockwise = up a 5th, one more sharp. C → G is five notes up (C D E F G). G has 1 sharp, D has 2, and so on.
  • Counterclockwise = up a 4th, one more flat. C → F → B♭ → E♭ ...
  • Inner ring = relative minor. Same notes and same key signature as the major key above it. Its home note is the 6th note of the major scale (C major → A minor).
  • Neighbors are close friends. Keys next to each other share 6 of their 7 notes. That's why songs often move to a neighbor key.
  • The highlighted block of 6 is your key's chords. For a major key: I in the middle, IV to the left, V to the right, and ii, vi, iii just inside them. Only vii° is left out.
  • Order of sharps: F C G D A E B (Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle). Order of flats is the reverse: B E A D G C F.
  • Quick trick to name a key signature: with sharps, go one half step up from the last sharp (F♯ → G major). With flats, the second to last flat is the key (B♭ E♭ → E♭ major).
